**Insight (MockLLM):** the scaffolding handles every prompt variant safely, but a mock
cannot show whether a model *obeys* "never invent policy details". That question needs a
real model — see the next section, which overturns the assumption made here.

**Default prompt selected:** `v2_role_and_constraints`, used **only together with retrieval**
(concise `v3` is used adaptively, see Phase 7 feedback-driven switching in the `plan_and_retrieve`
graph node). The justification is *not* that v2 prevents fabrication — the real-model run
below shows it does not.

## Real-Model Prompt Comparison (`gpt-4o-mini`, same 3 questions, no retrieval)

Generated by `scripts/generate_comparisons.py` with a live key (raw JSON in
`state/prompt_comparison_openai.json`). Ground truth from the knowledge base: returns are
30 days from delivery; standard shipping is **3-5 business days**; there is **no documented**
price-match policy.

| Question | v1_basic | v2_role_and_constraints | v3_role_constraints_concise | What Improved / Worsened |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "What is your return policy?" | "generally... usually 30 days... may not be eligible [for] final sale"; tells user to check the website | "typically ... 30 days of receiving your order... excluded items such as personalized or final sale" | "return most items within 30 days of receipt for a full refund... Some exceptions may apply, such as final sale items" | **Roughly right but unsupported.** All three guess "30 days" and invent exclusions (final sale). v3 is the shortest. None cites a source. |
| "How long does shipping take?" | "Standard 3 to 7 business days... International 1 to 4 weeks" | "standard shipping takes about **5-7** business days" | "standard shipping typically takes **3-7** business days" | **Wrong in all three** (actual 3-5). v1 and v3 are closest; v2 is furthest. No variant fixed it. International timing in v1 is invented. |
| "Do you price match competitors?" | Hedged: "depends on the company... contact them" | "**Our policy does not include price matching**" | "**We do not offer price matching**" | **v2/v3 worsened on safety.** They assert a specific policy that is not documented (the KB only says none is *documented*). v1's hedge was the safest answer, though unhelpful. |

**What improved:** v2/v3 speak as the retailer, offer escalation (v2), and v3 is concise.
**What worsened:** the "never invent policy details" constraint did not stop invention; in the
price-matching case the constrained prompts were *more* confidently wrong than the plain one.

**Conclusion:** prompt wording controls tone and length but cannot supply or verify facts, and
can even raise unwarranted confidence. Correctness has to come from retrieval grounding and
deterministic rules, as the next section shows.

## With vs Without Retrieval (Phase 4)

| Question | Without Retrieval | With Retrieval | Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| "What is your return policy?" | "I don't have specific documentation on that topic, so I don't want to guess..." | "Based on our policy documentation: [return_policy] Customers may return most items within 30 days of the delivery date for a full refund..." | Grounded, correct, specific answer instead of a generic non-answer. |
| "Is there a warranty on electronics?" | Generic non-answer | "[warranty_policy] Most electronics products include a 1-year manufacturer warranty covering defects in materials and workmanship..." | Same — real policy content surfaced. |
| "Do you price match competitors?" | Generic non-answer | "[faq] Q: Do you price-match competitors? A: This assistant does not have documentation confirming a price-match policy..." | Retrieval correctly finds the FAQ entry confirming there is *no* such policy — the agent states this precisely instead of refusing vaguely. |

**Conclusion (MockLLM):** retrieval measurably improves both correctness and specificity while
preserving the "don't fabricate" safety property established in Phase 3 — when nothing
relevant is retrieved, the agent still falls back to "I don't have documentation" instead
of guessing (see `docs/03_evaluation_report.md`, TC7).

## With vs Without Retrieval — Real Model (`gpt-4o-mini`)

Raw JSON: `state/rag_comparison_openai.json`.

| Question | Without retrieval | With retrieval | What improved |
|---|---|---|---|
| "What is your return policy?" | Generic: "generally allows items to be returned within 30 days of receipt... or exchange... tags intact" (adds an exchange option and conditions not in policy) | "30 days of the delivery date for a full refund to the original payment method... measured from the delivered date, not the order date" | Specific and correct: adds the refund-to-original-payment rule and the delivered-date window that the unaided answer lacked, and drops the invented "exchange" option. |
| "Is there a warranty on electronics?" | "Yes, most electronics come with a manufacturer's warranty... duration varies" | "Yes... a **1-year** manufacturer warranty... does not cover accidental damage, water damage, or normal wear and tear" | Concrete, documented terms replace vague generalities. |
| "Do you price match competitors?" | "**Our policy does not include price matching with competitors**" (fabricated) | "I do not have documentation confirming a price-match policy. I recommend connecting with a human agent" | **Fabrication eliminated.** Same model, same prompt; only grounding changed. |

**Conclusion:** the single largest quality and safety gain in the project comes from retrieval
grounding, not prompt engineering. With retrieval the real model states the documented
facts and, when nothing is documented, says so and offers a human instead of inventing.
